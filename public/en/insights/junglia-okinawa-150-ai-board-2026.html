Transcript

Read the full transcript

This AI board session examines the case of growing JUNGLIA OKINAWA toward 1.5 million annual visitors while protecting satisfaction. The 1.5 million figure is not a target formally announced by the operating company.

The analysis distinguishes official announcements, press coverage, third-party material, field observation from a single visit by Synapse Arrows, hypotheses, and unverified points. A single visit cannot be generalised.

Visitor numbers cannot be compared unless the period and the scope match. It is important not to reach a conclusion while differences remain — park and spa combined, repeat entries, a first year versus six months.

Okinawa as a whole receives large tourist inflows, but that cannot be treated directly as the addressable audience for a theme park in the north. Break it down into northern accommodation, means of travel, itinerary, travelling companions and time spent.

The official guidance gives roughly 90 minutes by car from Naha Airport or central Naha, and about 30 minutes from the Churaumi Aquarium. It is not only the journey in: the journey home after a day of activity also affects the value of the experience.

The target customer is not one group. Guests staying in the north, guests staying in Naha, families, short-visit tourists and repeat visitors each face different barriers and expect different value.

Candidate bottlenecks for growth are not only awareness: difficulty fitting the park into an itinerary, anxiety about transport, the journey after the experience, and how the value of the stay is explained. Use data to narrow down which is the primary constraint.

Existing measures include the official hotels, JUNGLIA EXPRESS, combination tickets with the Churaumi Aquarium, the roughly three-hour Furatto ticket, and Night Fes. Any proposal must not ignore these, and should check take-up and what remains unmet.

Additional hypotheses worth examining include integration with northern accommodation, an evening experience timed to the journey home, repeat visits driven by events, and product design that includes booking the transport. Demand and operational capacity both need verification.

A 90-day experiment should limit the target segment and the inventory on sale, and measure purchase rate, attendance rate, transport delays, time spent, satisfaction, additional spend and complaints. Record the metrics that got worse, not only the good numbers.

The provisional conclusion is to design growth around the unit of experience — a day in the north including the journey home — rather than chasing visitor counts alone. A final judgement would require the operator's non-public data.

FAQ

Does JUNGLIA OKINAWA have an official target of 1.5 million visitors a year?

Not in the public primary sources this article checked. The 1.5 million figure is a case setting adopted in the video, informed by a break-even figure cited in press coverage.

What are the main growth questions?

Not attendance volume alone, but travel to northern Okinawa, fitting the park into an itinerary, time spent, accommodation tie-ins, the journey home after the experience, and reasons to return — considered as one whole.

How should the proposals be verified?

Through a 90-day experiment with a narrow target segment and limited inventory, measuring purchase, attendance, transport, time spent, satisfaction, additional spend and complaints together.
