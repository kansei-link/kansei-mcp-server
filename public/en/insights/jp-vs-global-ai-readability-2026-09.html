We measured Japanese and global SaaS the same way — in this sample the gap sits in one place

The assumption that Japanese SaaS lags on AI readiness is not what the measurement shows. Most checks came out close, and one came out consistently apart.

We ran the same check over 162 Japanese and 84 global SaaS domains, then compared accounting against accounting, HR against HR so that a difference in category mix could not masquerade as a difference between markets. In this sample the gap concentrates in llms.txt adoption; everything else is close.

The comparison below uses the 8 categories with at least 5 domains on both sides (98 Japanese, 55 global). Categories too thin on either side were left out, because a single company moves the rate too far. The full 162 and 84 are not the base of the conclusion.

Where the gap sits

llms.txt — the plain text file telling an AI which pages explain what. The Japanese side was higher (i.e. worse) in every one of the 8 compared categories.

What this can and cannot say

This is a descriptive comparison of the sites we selected. It carries the real composition of each market and does not match companies on size — the global side holds more large vendors.

So "there is a gap in this sample" is supportable; "because it is Japan" is not. Nothing in this design separates market practice from company size. Doing that would need stratification or matching.

The 8 categories agreeing in direction is a clear observation, but categories are not independent trials — they share market conditions — so we do not treat that agreement as evidence of statistical significance.

And we are not claiming llms.txt works

What we measured is adoption, not effect. Whether publishing llms.txt makes an AI more likely to surface you is a separate question, and we have no evidence for it either way.

What we can say is that the difference concentrates here, in the same direction across every compared category, and that the file costs one text file to publish. A small effect and a small cost is still a decision you can make.

If you read an article claiming llms.txt gets you found by AI, ask for the evidence. We do not have it.

FAQ

Does publishing llms.txt get you found by AI?

This study does not answer that. We measured how many sites publish it, not what happens when they do. We have no evidence of an effect, and neither, as far as we can tell, does anyone else. What we can say is that this is where the difference sits and that the file is cheap to publish.

Does this mean Japanese SaaS is behind?

Not from this. In this sample sitemap.xml was missing less often on the Japanese side, and structured data and contact details differed by under ten points. It is also a comparison of selected sites rather than of markets.

Why not just compare the overall rates?

The two samples have different category mixes — the Japanese side is heavier on accounting and HR, the global side on marketing — so an overall rate would report that mix as a market difference. We compare within categories and average the category rates instead.

Do you publish per-company scores?

No. This is a lightweight readability check, not the ARI rating, and attaching company names would blur the two. Only the distribution is published.

Method and limits

Measured 2026-09-03 using the same public API as the free check on this site, so the figures are reproducible with the same tool. The Japanese sample is the 162 domains resolved from the 200 services verified for ARI Award 2026 Summer; the global sample is the 84 business SaaS domains in KanseiLink's database with a resolvable product domain. Comparison uses the 8 categories with at least 5 domains on both sides, via category rates and their unweighted mean.

Limits: the samples are unequal (162 against 84) and per-category the global side runs n=5-12, so one company moves a rate noticeably. The two were not selected by the same rule. The global side skews toward large vendors, so company size cannot be separated from market. This is one point in time and sites change. The check measures machine readability; it is not a quality judgement and not the ARI rating.

What we do not promise: we cannot guarantee visibility or ranking inside any AI product, and we do not treat a single AI answer as market share. Principles of Independence
